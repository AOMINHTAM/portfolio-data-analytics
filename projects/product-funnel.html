Product Performance & Conversion Funnel Analysis

Role: Data Analyst (Personal Analytics Project) Timeline: Sep 2026 Stack: Python 3.x, Pandas, NumPy, Plotly (Funnel), Seaborn, Matplotlib Scope: 32,313 Orders • 472,871 Sessions • 1,188,124 Pageviews

1. Dataset, Scope & Business Scenario

Project Type Personal Project / Simulated Business Case (Maven Fuzzy Factory)
Dataset Source Maven Fuzzy Factory Teaching Dataset (6 CSVs: orders, items, refunds, products, sessions, pageviews)
Data Scale 32,313 orders • 472,871 sessions • 1,188,124 pageviews
Time Range & Currency Mar 2012 – Mar 2015 (3 Years) • USD ($)

To investigate a drop-off gap between top-of-funnel site traffic and completed revenue, this analysis audits product-level conversion steps and refund risks across the browsing-to-purchase journey.

  • Calculate Net Revenue After Refunds (Gross Revenue - Refund Amount) across all catalog items.
  • Construct sequential session-based conversion funnels tracking unique sessions across milestones: Catalog (/products) → Product Detail Page (PDP) → Cart (/cart) → Order Confirmation (/thank-you-for-your-order).
  • Compare conversion performance between flagship SKUs: The Original Mr. Fuzzy vs. The Forever Love Bear.
Denominator & Funnel Definitions (Verified in Code): Each milestone counts deduplicated website_session_ids (groupby.max()) progressing sequentially from /products (261,231 sessions): Mr. Fuzzy (162,525 PDP → 69,957 Cart → 23,861 Order Confirmation, 34.1% Cart-to-Order) vs. Love Bear (26,033 PDP → 14,485 Cart → 4,803 Order Confirmation, 33.2% Cart-to-Order). Sessions landing directly on a PDP without /products are outside this catalog-anchored funnel, and refunds are deducted separately in SKU Net Revenue rather than removed from clickstream progression.

2. Key Findings & The "Love Bear" Paradox

  • The "Love Bear" Paradox:
    • Top-of-Funnel (Catalog → PDP): Mr. Fuzzy captures 62.2% of catalog sessions, while Love Bear captures only 10.0%—indicating substantially lower catalog-page click-through exposure (placement hypothesis to test).
    • Mid-Funnel (PDP → Cart): Once a user views Love Bear, it converts at a higher PDP-to-Cart rate (55.6%) than Mr. Fuzzy (43.0%), making it a high-intent candidate for merchandising visibility tests.
  • Cart-to-Order Drop-off Bottleneck: Across both product lines, progression from /cart to /thank-you-for-your-order drops by ~66% (~33–34% completion).
  • Refund Rate Anomaly: While Mr. Fuzzy is the primary net revenue driver, The Birthday Sugar Panda exhibits the highest refund rate in the catalog, flagging it as a priority candidate for product quality and supplier investigation rather than a proven supplier defect.

3. Technical QA & Engineering Refactoring

  • Interactive Funnel Architecture: Migrated legacy Matplotlib horizontal bar plots to plotly.graph_objects.Funnel, enabling interactive hover states and step-to-step drop-off ratios.
  • Typecasting Optimization: Replaced repeated datetime casting with a unified ingestion loop utilizing pd.to_datetime().
  • Dependency Deprecation Fixes: Modernized Seaborn plotting parameters (hue='product_name', legend=False) to eliminate deprecation warnings and ensure compatibility with future releases.

4. Proposed Experiments & Recommendations

  • Traffic Injection via Cross-Selling: Place Love Bear as a "Frequently Bought Together" widget on Mr. Fuzzy's high-traffic product page to test catalog-to-PDP lift.
  • Checkout UX Overhaul: Audit hidden shipping costs and guest checkout friction to address the observed 66% cart-to-order abandonment rate.
  • Automated Abandoned-Cart Recovery: Pilot automated email/SMS recovery reminders at 1 hour and 24 hours post-abandonment.
Validation Plan: All UX and recovery changes should be evaluated via A/B testing measuring Cart-to-Order Completion Rate, Net Revenue per Session, and Refund Rate (monitoring quality controls on higher-refund items such as Birthday Sugar Panda).
