CUSTOMER ANALYTICS • CASE STUDY

Customer 360 & RFM Segmentation Analysis

Role: Data Analyst (Simulated Business Case) Timeline: Sep 2026 Stack: Python 3.x, Pandas, NumPy, Squarify, Seaborn, Matplotlib Scope: 51,290 Line-Items (25,728 Unique Orders) / 17,415 Customers

1. Dataset, Scope & Business Scenario

Project Type Personal Project / Simulated Business Case ("CosmeticWorld")
Dataset Source Simulated E-Commerce CSVs (EcomSales, Customer, Product, Region)
Scale & Granularity 51,290 transaction line-items • 25,728 unique orders • 17,415 unique customers
Time Range & Currency Jan 2020 – Dec 2023 (4 Years) • Simulated USD ($)

In this simulated CosmeticWorld scenario, marketing leadership needed to restructure annual promotional spend for a New Year Rejuvenation Campaign, shifting from broad-spectrum discounting toward targeted lifecycle retention. Note that 51,290 represents order line-items across 25,728 distinct OrderIDs.

  • Quantify customer behavior across Recency, Frequency, and Monetary dimensions using quantile scoring (pd.qcut).
  • Classify the customer base into actionable operational cohorts (Champions, Loyal Customers, Cant Lose Them, Hibernating, Lost).
  • Construct a Customer 360 Profile analyzing demographic, geographic, and basket affinities.
  • Suppress low-yield outreach on dormant accounts to prioritize budget allocation toward higher-retention tiers.
Methodological Definitions (Verified in Code): Snapshot Date: 2024-01-01 (max(OrderDate) + 1 day) • Recency: Days since latest order to snapshot date • Frequency: Distinct OrderID count (nunique, preventing line-item inflation) • Monetary: Gross Sales sum in simulated USD (the dataset has no return/refund or ad-spend/CAC tables) • Scoring: Quintile pd.qcut(q=5) (with .rank(method="first") tie-breaking on Frequency) mapped via deterministic R/F/M threshold rules into 8 segments.

3. Customer 360 View: Demographic & Basket Affinities

  • Core Demographics: Peak gross sales (>60%) are driven by ages 25 to 49. High correlation between degree holders (Bachelor’s/Graduate) averaging $60k–$80k income and top Average Order Value (AOV).
  • Geographic Hubs: Asia Pacific and Europe serve as the primary revenue drivers in the simulated dataset, outpacing USCA and LATAM markets.
  • Basket Preferences: Champions heavily prioritize premium self-care lines: Nail Care, Hand Creams, and Body Moisturizers.

4. Proposed Campaign Experiments & Evaluation Plan

  • Hypothesis — Weighted Budget Focus (70/30 Pilot): Prioritize ~70% of promotional spend as a controlled pilot toward Champions (loyalty perks) and Cant Lose Them (win-back reactivation vouchers, $653k historical pool), while keeping 30% for nurturing Big Spenders and New / Promising tiers.
  • Hypothesis — Dormant Outreach Suppression: Pause higher-cost SMS/direct mail to Lost and Hibernating tiers (5,181 combined accounts) to reduce low-yield promotional outreach spend, testing low-cost email-only reactivation instead.
  • Hypothesis — Cross-Selling Bridge: Offer top-affinity subcategories (Nail Care & Hand Creams) as Gift-With-Purchase incentives for New / Promising buyers to test graduation into Loyal Customers.
Observed Insight vs. Business Hypothesis: The revenue concentration (<29% generating >53% revenue) and demographic profiles (ages 25–49 driving >60% gross sales) are empirically observed from the 2020–2023 dataset. The proposed 70/30 split is an initial testing hypothesis rather than a proven optimum—requiring an A/B holdout test tracking channel CAC, Incremental Gross Margin, Reactivation Rate, and 60-day Repeat Purchase Rate before scaling.
